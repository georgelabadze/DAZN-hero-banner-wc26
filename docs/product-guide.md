# Product Guide: DAZN Hero Banner WC26

## Core principle
- The hero should tell one clear story at a time
- Every module that is switched on should improve clarity, hierarchy, or confidence
- If a module repeats information already visible elsewhere, it should usually be removed
- Shorter content stacks travel better across desktop, tablet, and mobile

## Approved modules
- Copy limits
- Title
- Subtitle
- Logo
- Label
- Helper text

## Copy limits
- Larger title: up to about `32` characters including spaces
- Large title: up to about `48` characters including spaces
- Subtitle: about `70` characters
- Label: about `24` characters
- Helper text: about `72` characters

## Title
- Use when:
  - the hero needs one clear message that can carry the promotion on its own
- Avoid when:
  - the headline becomes too long, wraps awkwardly, or needs too much supporting explanation
- Impact:
  - the title defines the hierarchy of the whole content stack and controls how much room remains for the modules below it

## Subtitle
- Use when:
  - one supporting line of context helps the title land faster
- Avoid when:
  - it repeats the headline, becomes body copy, or pushes the stack too deep
- Impact:
  - it adds clarity, but it is also the easiest module to overuse; shorter copy keeps the banner cleaner across all breakpoints

## Logo
- Use when:
  - the property or event brand adds recognition and helps the hero scan faster
- Avoid when:
  - the creative is already busy or already contains logos, badges, or detailed marks
- Impact:
  - it adds brand context above the label, but it also increases visual density in the top part of the content stack

## Label
- Use when:
  - a short event date, season range, promo note, or offer cue improves scanning
- Avoid when:
  - the title already says the same thing or the message is too long to stay lightweight
- Impact:
  - it introduces context without changing the main hierarchy, as long as it stays short and useful

## Helper text
- Use when:
  - extra conditions, rights notes, or legal clarifications are required but should stay secondary to the CTA
- Avoid when:
  - the text is non-essential and only adds visual weight
- Impact:
  - it extends the vertical footprint of the bottom content stack, so it should only be used when the extra context is genuinely necessary

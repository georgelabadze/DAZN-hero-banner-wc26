Live layout behavior

  • Width always follows the parent container.
  • The listed ratio is the target, not a hard lock at every screen height.
  • Desktop remains a `16:9` inset card and still caps against the viewport after the `64px` header.
  • Desktop title reads at roughly `50%` of the hero width, while subtitle and helper text read at roughly `30%`.
  • Tablet and mobile both use the header fixed over the creative from first paint.
  • Tablet targets a true `5:7` full-width creative frame and uses an `80%` title width with `60%` subtitle and helper widths.
  • Mobile targets `9:16` creative and keeps the current centered text widths.
  • Tablet and mobile drop the card border, radius, and glow chrome so the small-screen experience stays clean and immersive.
  • Tablet and mobile also use a stronger top scrim so the overlaid header stays readable against the art.
  • Tablet caps the live hero at about `85%` of the viewport, while mobile caps at about `90%` in normal cases, but both now keep stronger minimum heights in short or rotated viewports.
  • When tablet or mobile gets short, the hero stops shrinking past its floor and extra page scroll is expected instead.
  • Live media keeps the full focus position on taller viewports, but when the hero becomes height-constrained it preserves only horizontal framing from data and crops from the bottom.

Do

Keep the top breathing zone visually calm

Leave space under the header for cleaner separation. Soft background, negative space, or low-contrast detail works best here.

Don't

Place faces, logos, or hard contrast directly under the header

If the top edge is too busy, the hero feels cramped and the artwork competes with the page chrome before the main message even starts.

Do

Place the main subject higher than the live content stack

Faces, trophies, logos, and key action should sit above the protected text zone so the hero can hold title, subtitle, and CTA cleanly.

Don't

Fill the lower content-safe area with bright or dense detail

The scrim helps, but it should not be the only thing saving readability.

Do

Deliver matching stills and posters for every motion asset

The poster is part of the experience, not a fallback afterthought.

Don't

Add the top logo when the artwork is already busy

If the creative already carries marks, badges, or fine detail, keep the live stack lighter.

Product

Use the modules deliberately so the hero tells one clear story at a time.

The live demo exposes multiple switches, but in product usage each one should answer a specific communication need rather than simply adding more surface area to the banner.

Copy limits

  • Larger title: up to about `32` characters including spaces.
  • Large title: up to about `48` characters including spaces.
  • Subtitle: about `70` characters, assuming the creative tolerates up to 3 lines on desktop.
  • Label: about `24` characters.
  • Helper text: about `72` characters.

Title

Use when
Keep the title as the main message of the hero and use the larger size only for short, punchy headlines.
Avoid when
The title becomes too long, wraps awkwardly, or starts fighting the artwork for attention.
Impact
The title defines the hierarchy of the entire content stack and directly affects how much room is left for the subtitle below it.

Subtitle

Use when
Add one supporting layer of context that helps the title land without overloading it.
Avoid when
The subtitle repeats the title, becomes body copy, or pushes the stack too deep for the creative.
Impact
The safe area supports up to 3 lines on desktop, but shorter copy keeps the banner cleaner across all breakpoints.

Logo

Purpose
Add event or property branding above the label.
Use when
The logo adds recognition without crowding the art.
Avoid when
The artwork already contains logos, badges, or small detailed marks.
Impact
Adds brand signal but increases visual density in the stack.

Label

Purpose
Surface the event date range, promo note, or discount message.
Use when
A short timestamp or offer cue improves scanning.
Avoid when
The title already carries the same information.
Impact
Adds a small pre-title module without changing the full hierarchy.

Helper text

Purpose
Handle conditions, rights notes, or legal clarification.
Use when
Extra context is required but should stay secondary.
Avoid when
The text is non-essential and only adds visual weight.
Impact
Extends the vertical footprint of the bottom content stack.
